Find the class that fits

Six classes, from first days at school to the end of Class III. Lessons are in English. Early years are full of talk, songs, and play. Primary is reading, writing, and maths, still with one teacher for the class.

Early years

Nursery

Songs, stories, and friends.

Your child learns how a school day works: listening, playing, and speaking in a group. The work is mostly talk, songs, and play, with a teacher who stays close. You can see the first habits of school starting.

LKG

Letters, numbers, and my name.

Children begin to write their names, hear stories read aloud, and count everyday things. The year is still full of play, with a clearer routine a parent can ask about.

UKG

I can try it myself.

More reading and counting, and a longer stretch of the day done without a hand on the shoulder. Parents see a child getting ready for the paper work of Class I.

Primary

Class I

I can read a little.

Short passages, words and sentences, and number work on paper. This is the first primary year. The teacher is still close, and parents can ask what to practise at home.

Class II

My writing tells a story.

Longer reading, writing that holds a full idea, and maths that builds on Class I. Parents can see the work getting steadier from one week to the next.

Class III

I am ready for what comes next.

Steadier reading, writing, and number work, so children leave this class ready for the next school year. The teacher will tell you how your child is getting on before the year ends.

What you can ask the teacher

These are fair questions at a visit, or once your child has a place.

  • What the class is working on this term, in reading, writing, and number work.
  • How your child joins in with the other children.
  • What to practise at home, and what can wait.
  • Who to call if your child will be absent, or if something at home has changed.
